Oh, the day I met your grandfather. Well. That would have been — oh goodness, 1953? No, wait, it had to be 1935 because Eleanor was already married by then and her wedding was the summer before. Anyway. There was a dance at St. Casimir's, the parish hall, they had them every month and Ruth — that's Ruth Kowalski, she lived two doors down from us on Mitchell Street, her mother made the most beautiful pierogi, we used to stand in her kitchen — anyway Ruth dragged me to this dance because she was sweet on a boy named Stanley and she didn't want to go alone. I didn't even want to go. I had a cold. My mother said the air would do me good, which made no sense at all, but you didn't argue with my mother. So we get there and it's crowded and hot and I'm standing by the coat racks feeling sorry for myself, and this young man comes over — and I thought he was coming to talk to Ruth, because everyone always came to talk to Ruth — and he asks ME to dance. Me! With my red nose. And I said, I remember exactly, I said "I have a terrible cold, you'll catch it," and he said "then I'll have something of yours to keep." Fresh! I thought he was so fresh. But I danced with him. He was a terrible dancer, God love him, he stepped on my foot twice and apologized both times like he'd broken it. His name was Henry. Your grandfather. And Ruth married her Stanley, too, the next spring, so I suppose that dance worked out for everybody. Oh — and I found out later he'd asked the band to play a slow one. He never admitted it but Stanley told Ruth and Ruth told me. Fifty-two years and he never admitted it.
